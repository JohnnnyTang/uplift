# Teaching a moved spring

Invite a source placement on the other hollow, then observe which surface becomes wet. Moving the stable-id spring replaces its inlet; existing water is retained. Blue encodes actual model water depth and ink dots mark actual wet samples. Neither is a parcel trace, velocity indicator or precomputed route.

The prepared twin troughs have one outlet on the left downstream boundary; other edges are closed. Do not ask for symmetric outlet destinations. This lesson makes only the registered source-location/wet-extent claim, at the tested fixed-step horizon and tested prepared slopes. It does not establish a general watershed rule. Terrain is fixed: erosion, sediment transport, rainfall and evaporation are disabled. Infiltration is not modelled. Time is uncalibrated and no geological years appear.

Source placement automatically gives a bounded real model response. Reduced motion computes the same steps and shows the settled result without animated intermediate frames. Keyboard: focus the spring button and use arrow keys. Reset restores the authored source arrangement and dry terrain; it pauses and keeps reading level. A labelled static map is available when live simulation/rendering is unavailable and does not count as a run.

Source: [Mei, Decaudin & Hu (2007), Fast Hydraulic Erosion Simulation and Visualization on GPU](https://www-evasion.imag.fr/Publications/2007/MDH07/).
