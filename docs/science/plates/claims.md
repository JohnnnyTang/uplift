# Claim register and "What this shows / ignores" (session 7A → 7E)

7E writes the lesson copy against this register, as lesson 1 did against
[its register](../glacial/lesson/README.md). Every claim is scientific text for expert review: it is drafted,
marked `<!-- expert-review -->`, and cited (keys in [sources.md](sources.md)). The wording is a draft; 7E
may shorten it but may not strengthen it. Level: **S** = school (the default level), **U** = university
(the deeper level).

**The rule above every claim.** The scene is an illustration. No claim may say or imply that a landform
forms, grows, is computed or is simulated in the scene. Real processes are described as happening in the
world ("when two continents collide, the crust is pushed up"); the scene is described as showing their
shapes ("the drawing shows a range like the Himalaya").

## What this shows / ignores (draft)

7E finalizes this list. The page carries only the one label line, _Illustration: shapes drawn from real
examples_; this list is the fuller account behind it.

### School level

**This illustration shows**

- the three kinds of plate boundary between two continents, and the typical shape each one makes: a
  mountain range where plates collide, a rift valley where they pull apart, and sideways offsets where they
  slide past each other;
- the crust on the front face: thicker under the range, thinner under the rift, unchanged at the
  transform;
- a sequence from early to late, which you can play, pause and scrub.

**It leaves out or simplifies**

- **The shapes are drawn, not simulated.** They were authored from real examples (the Himalaya and Tibet,
  the East African Rift, the San Andreas at Wallace Creek). Nothing forms in the scene, and the "stage"
  slider is not a clock and shows no years.
- **No erosion.** Real mountains are cut by rivers and glaciers, and real rifts fill with sediment and
  lakes. Here the land is only raised, lowered or moved.
- **Not to scale.** Heights and rock layers are stretched {{scene.surfaceVerticalExaggeration}} times;
  the crust base is drawn at {{scene.crustVerticalExaggeration}} times. The range is narrower than the
  Himalaya, and the sideways offset is much larger than Wallace Creek's and much smaller than the whole
  fault's.
- **Simple shapes.** Real rifts are usually lopsided, with one main fault; real ranges are lopsided too,
  with a high plateau behind. Real plate boundaries are zones of many faults, not one line.
- **Left out on purpose:** volcanoes and magma, earthquakes, oceans, and one plate sinking under another.
- **The rock layers are made up:** generic layers with natural colours, not a map of real rocks.

### University additions

- The root follows Airy isostasy with crust 2,800 and mantle 3,300 kg/m³ (root ≈ 5.6 × height), applied
  directly under the range. Real ranges are supported partly by the bending strength of the plate, which
  spreads the root more widely.
- The rift's thin crust is not balanced in the same way: real rift floors also depend on hot, low-density
  mantle beneath them (Kel94 reports very low upper-mantle velocities under the Kenya Rift), which the
  drawing does not show.
- The crust is one band with a sharp base (the Moho); the upper and lower crust, and the mantle part of
  the plate, are not distinguished.
- The two sides of the transform move as rigid blocks with a narrow zone of drag between them; the real San
  Andreas zone is in places tens of kilometres wide.
- The page's figures (heights, widths, thicknesses, offsets, plate speeds) are real-world values from the
  sources, not measurements of the drawing.
