Lesson 2 · Plate tectonics

Where plates meet

Lithospheric plates move apart at divergent boundaries, together at convergent ones and past each other at transform ones.

Between continents, collision thickens the crust beneath a high range, stretching thins it beneath a faulted rift, and a transform moves the land sideways without raising or lowering it.

Choose a boundary, then compare all three, crust included, at the same stage.

Under a high collision range, how deep does the crust's root reach?
Lift the flap

Several times deeper. Thick, light crust floats on the denser mantle, like an iceberg in water (isostasy): the root reaches about 5.6 times as deep as the range stands high (TS14; Fia).

On the front face the root looks only about as deep as the range is high: heights are drawn ×4, crust depths ×0.75. Real crust beneath southern Tibet is about twice the usual thickness (Nab09; CM95).

plate
a large piece of the lithosphere, moving slowly relative to its neighbours
plate boundary
where two plates meet: in reality a zone of many faults, not one line
convergent
plates moving together; between continents, collision thickens the crust
divergent
plates moving apart; a stretched continent breaks into a faulted rift valley
transform
plates sliding past each other; crust is neither made nor destroyed
crust
the outermost rock layer, above the denser mantle; its base is the Moho
