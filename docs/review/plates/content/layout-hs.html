Lesson 2 · Plate tectonics

Where plates meet

Earth's outer shell is broken into huge plates that move very slowly.

Where two plates meet, they push together, pull apart or slide past each other, and each kind of boundary shapes the land in its own way.

Choose a boundary and press Play to see the landform it makes, from early to late.

When two continents push together, what happens to the crust under the mountains?
Lift the flap

Thicker. When two continents collide, neither sinks, because continental rock is light. The crust crumples and piles up, so under the mountains it is much thicker than elsewhere: a deep root.

Choose the convergent boundary and look at the front face: the drawing shows the crust thicker under the range, as it is under the real Himalaya.
